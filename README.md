# Customer Shopping Behavior Analysis | End-to-End Data Analytics Project

**Python • PostgreSQL • Power BI • Exploratory Data Analysis • Customer Segmentation**

## Project Overview

This end-to-end data analytics project explores **3,900 customer shopping records** to understand purchasing patterns, customer segments, product preferences, and subscription behavior. It combines data cleaning and feature engineering in Python, business-focused SQL analysis in PostgreSQL, and an interactive Power BI dashboard to support retail decision-making.

## Business Problem

A retail company wants to understand how demographics, discounts, reviews, shipping preferences, and purchasing history influence shopping behavior. The objective is to identify trends that can help improve customer engagement, product positioning, marketing strategies, and long-term loyalty.

**Key business question:** How can the company use shopping data to identify trends, improve customer engagement, and optimize marketing and product strategies?

## Dataset

- **Records:** 3,900 purchases
- **Original columns:** 18
- **Customer attributes:** Age, gender, location, subscription status
- **Purchase attributes:** Item purchased, category, purchase amount, season, size, color
- **Behavioral attributes:** Discounts, promo codes, previous purchases, purchase frequency, review ratings, shipping type
- **Missing values identified:** 37 entries in `Review Rating`

## Project Workflow

### 1. Data Preparation & Exploratory Analysis (Python)

- Inspected dataset structure, descriptive statistics, and missing values.
- Filled missing review ratings using the **median rating within each product category**.
- Standardized column names to `snake_case`.
- Created an `age_group` field for demographic analysis.
- Mapped purchase-frequency categories to approximate day intervals in `purchase_frequency_days`.
- Checked the relationship between `discount_applied` and `promo_code_used`, then removed the redundant promo-code field.
- Loaded the prepared data into PostgreSQL for further analysis.

### 2. Business Analysis (PostgreSQL)

SQL queries addressed the following questions:

1. How much revenue is generated by male and female customers?
2. Which customers used discounts but still spent at or above the average purchase amount?
3. Which five products have the highest average review ratings?
4. How does average purchase amount compare between Standard and Express shipping?
5. How do subscribers and non-subscribers compare in average spend and total revenue?
6. Which products have the highest proportion of discounted purchases?
7. How many customers belong to New, Returning, and Loyal segments?
8. What are the three most-purchased products in each category?
9. How are repeat buyers distributed across subscription statuses?
10. Which age groups contribute the most revenue?

**SQL techniques used:** `GROUP BY`, `CASE WHEN`, aggregate functions, subqueries, common table expressions (CTEs), and `ROW_NUMBER()` window functions.

### 4. Business Recommendations

Based on the analysis, the project report recommends:

- **Subscription growth:** Promote benefits that encourage customers to subscribe.
- **Customer retention:** Reward repeat purchasers and encourage progression toward loyal-customer status.
- **Discount optimization:** Review discount strategy to balance sales growth and margins.
- **Product marketing:** Highlight highly rated and frequently purchased products.
- **Targeted campaigns:** Focus marketing on relevant customer age groups and shipping preferences.

These are recommendations from the project report, not claims of measured business impact.
